# The Island

The island was small enough to walk around before breakfast, and Clara had done so every morning for as long as she
could remember. She knew each rock by its shape and each tide pool by the creatures that lived in it, and she could tell
from the color of the water what kind of day the sea intended to have.

Her father kept the light. He had kept it for twenty-two years, through storms that tore the slates from the roof and
calms so still that the gulls seemed to hang in the air like paper birds on strings. He rarely spoke of the mainland,
and when he did it was in the careful voice of a man describing a country he had read about but never visited.

> There are two kinds of people, he told her once: those who look at the sea, and those who look at the shore. Keepers
> must be the first kind.

Clara was not sure which kind she was. On clear evenings she would climb to the gallery and watch the far coast turn
from green to violet to black, and count the lights of the town as they came on one by one. She would wonder who lit
them, and what those people said to one another at supper, and whether any of them ever looked out across the water and
wondered about her.

# The Letter

The letter came in the spring, with the first supply boat of the year. It was addressed to her, which had never happened
before, in a slanting hand she did not recognize. She carried it unopened to the top of the tower and sat with it in her
lap for a long time, watching the boat grow small on its way back to the shore.
