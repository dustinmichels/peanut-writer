# Introduction

Peanut Writer is a word processor that keeps **what you write** apart from **how it looks**. You write with a ribbon of
buttons, as in Word, and a template sets the finished pages beside you as you type. Your document holds only words and
structure (this is a heading, this is a figure), saved as plain text; the template holds every font, size, color and
margin. [Style apart from content](./style-and-content) explains why. The rest of the app follows from it.

It also does the bookkeeping: figures, tables and equations number themselves, cross-references keep up when things
move, and the table of contents writes itself.

## A quick tour

![The Peanut Writer window: the ribbon across the top, the editor on the left and the typeset page on the right](/screenshots/editor.png)

- **Type on the left.** Headings, lists, pictures and tables look like themselves. Every option is a ribbon button.
- **Read on the right.** The preview is the typeset page, exactly what your PDF will be.
- **Pick a look on the Design tab**, and change your mind any time. Your text stays as it is.
- **Caption pictures and tables** and they're numbered. Refer to them and the reference keeps up.
- **Export** to PDF, Word or a web page, or **Sync files** to keep a PDF and Word copy beside your document.

## Your first document

[Install Peanut Writer](./install) and open it. A start dialog shows **Create New**, **Open…**, **Open Sample**, your
recent documents, and links to these docs and GitHub. Closing it leaves a blank, untitled document; opening a document
from Finder skips it.

The sample (also **Help → Welcome Guide**) is a short guide written in Peanut Writer. Move its picture below the table
and watch both numbers change.

**Create New**, or **New** (⌘N) any time, asks for a name and a place and makes a `.peanut` package there for the
document, its pictures and its backups. See [Documents, saving and export](./documents).
